## Best Practices, Open-Access Tools, and Methodological Mistakes in Electricity Price Modeling

This paper identifies best practices for electricity price forecasting (EPF) model evaluation, introduces open-access tools and datasets, and highlights common methodological pitfalls in the existing literature.

### Checklist of Best Practices for Electricity Price Model Evaluation

The authors propose a set of best practices to ensure rigorous, reproducible, and meaningful EPF research:
- Test Period Length:
- The test dataset should comprise at least one year of data  (Lago et al., 2021). This ensures that forecasting methods are evaluated across the complete range of effects that occur throughout the year, including extreme events . Short test periods (e.g., four weeks) are problematic as they can lead to cherry-picking weeks where a method excels, may not be representative of year-round price behavior, and can neglect the impact of rare but significant electrical grid situations .
- Benchmark Models:
- Any new model must be compared against state-of-the-art open-access models  (Lago et al., 2021). The paper provides the LEAR (Lasso Estimated AutoRegressive) and DNN (Deep Neural Network) models as highly accurate and relatively simple benchmarks for comparison  .
- Statistical Tests (Diebold-Mariano, Giacomini-White):
- Statistical testing is mandatory to assess whether observed differences in performance are statistically significant  (Lago et al., 2021). The paper recommends using the Diebold-Mariano (DM) test and the Giacomini-White (GW) test, both of which are included in the epftoolbox library  . The DM test compares forecasts based on the null hypothesis of zero expected loss differential . The GW test is preferred for conditional predictive ability and accounts for parameter estimation uncertainty .
- Recalibration Scheme:
- Forecasting models should be recalibrated on a daily basis rather than being estimated once and then evaluated over the entire test dataset  (Lago et al., 2021). Daily recalibration ensures that models incorporate new market information, reflecting real-life conditions .
- Hyperparameter Optimization:
- Hyperparameters must be estimated using a validation dataset that is distinct from the test dataset  (Lago et al., 2021). This prevents overfitting and ensures fair evaluation .
- Data Handling and Transparency:
- The split and dates of the datasets (training, validation, test) must be explicitly stated  (Lago et al., 2021).
- All inputs used by the model must be explicitly defined  (Lago et al., 2021).
- The test dataset should always be selected as the last segment of the full dataset, without any overlap with training or validation data  (Lago et al., 2021).
- The employed datasets should be open-access  (Lago et al., 2021).
- Computational Cost:
- The computational cost of new methods should be evaluated and compared against existing methods  (Lago et al., 2021). Models with high computational requirements (e.g., taking more than 30 minutes to an hour for daily recalibration) may not be suitable for real-time spot market forecasting .

### Open-Access Tools and Datasets Provided by the Paper

The authors provide the following open-access resources through the epftoolbox library:
- Benchmark Datasets: Five datasets representing different day-ahead electricity markets (Nord Pool, PJM, EPEX-BE, EPEX-FR, EPEX-DE) are provided, each comprising six years of data  (Lago et al., 2021) . These datasets include hourly day-ahead prices and two influential exogenous variables for each market .
- State-of-the-Art Models: Implementations of the LEAR and DNN models are freely available as part of the epftoolbox  (Lago et al., 2021). These models are highly accurate and relatively simple, making them suitable benchmarks  .
- Forecasts: The library includes forecasts from the open-access methods across the five benchmark datasets, allowing researchers to make comparisons without re-training or re-estimating models  (Lago et al., 2021).
- Python Toolbox: An open-source Python library (epftoolbox) is designed to provide a common research framework for EPF, offering easy access to the data, models, and evaluation tools  (Lago et al., 2021) .

### Common Methodological Mistakes Identified in the Literature

The authors identify several recurring methodological issues in EPF research:
- Inadequate Testing Periods: Many studies use test periods that are too short (e.g., one-week or few-month periods), which can lead to inconclusive or biased results, especially regarding special days like holidays or rare extreme events  (Lago et al., 2021) .
- Lack of Reproducibility: Papers often fail to provide enough details to reproduce the research, such as not specifying the exact split between training and test datasets, not indicating the inputs used, or not specifying the dataset employed  (Lago et al., 2021) .
- Unfair Comparisons: New methods are frequently compared only with simpler statistical or machine learning methods, rather than against well-established or state-of-the-art algorithms, leading to potentially misleading conclusions about their performance  (Lago et al., 2021) .
- Data Contamination: Issues arise when parts of the training dataset are used for testing, or when the test dataset is randomly sampled from the full dataset, leading to potential overfitting and overestimated accuracy  (Lago et al., 2021) .
- Ex-post Hyperparameter Optimization: Optimizing hyperparameters using the test dataset (ex-post) leads to overfitting and grants the model an unfair advantage  (Lago et al., 2021).
- Lack of Recalibration: Models are often estimated once and then evaluated over the entire test dataset without daily recalibration, which does not reflect real-life conditions where new market information becomes available continuously  (Lago et al., 2021).
- Inadequate Metrics: The mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) is highlighted as an unreliable metric that can yield misleading results, particularly with prices close to zero  (Lago et al., 2021) .
- Lack of Statistical Significance Testing: Many studies only compare accuracy using error metrics without performing statistical tests to determine if observed differences are significant  (Lago et al., 2021).
In conclusion, the paper stresses the importance of robust evaluation practices, transparent data and code sharing, and the use of appropriate statistical tools to advance the field of electricity price forecasting. The epftoolbox is presented as a valuable resource to address these shortcomings.

## 4. 主要結果・命題（数値）
- DNN アンサンブルは5市場すべてで統計的に有意に最良（例外: NP で LEAR アンサンブルとの差は非有意）（6.2、p.16）。LEAR アンサンブルは個別 LEAR より有意に良い。計算時間（Table 4、p.16）: LEAR 1–10秒、LEAR アンサンブル 20–25秒、DNN 2–5分、DNN アンサンブル 8–20分 → LEAR は最大 100 倍安く、秒単位の意思決定では最良。
- 6.4.2（p.16）: MAE と rMAE は同等に信頼できる、sMAPE は MAPE より良いが rMAE に劣る、MAPE は誤った結論を導く、RMSE は EPF の損失構造を表さない。
- 6.4.1（p.16）: 電力購入費用は線形なので線形（絶対）誤差が適切。

## 6. 本研究との関係
- 引用予定箇所: 第5章(b) 価格過程モデルの検証作法、第10章。本研究は予測モデルではなくシミュレーション用の価格過程だが、Lago らのチェックリストのうち (1) ≥1年の検証期間、(6) rMAE などスケールフリー指標、(7) モデル変種比較（例: 3レジーム vs 1レジーム）での DM/GW 検定、(8) ローリング再推定、(10)–(12) 分割・入力の明示と時系列末尾での検証（データ汚染回避）を採用したことを明記する。
- 6.4.1 の「損失構造に合う指標を使う」に倣い、本研究では価格誤差より蓄電池収益（経済損失）ベースの比較を主指標とし、価格については rMAE を補助指標とする、と述べる。
